# AI-Based Energy Consumption Intelligence & Optimization System

## SDG 7 — Affordable and Clean Energy

### Problem Statement

Increasing electricity demand and inefficient energy consumption are major challenges for households, educational institutions, offices, commercial buildings, and other organizations.

Although electricity consumption data is increasingly available through smart meters, energy monitoring systems, and utility records, the availability of data alone does not provide users with a clear understanding of how energy is being consumed. Users often know **how much electricity was consumed**, but not necessarily **when consumption is abnormal, what usage patterns are causing high consumption, where energy may be getting wasted, or how consumption can be reduced without affecting essential activities**.

Traditional energy monitoring systems primarily focus on displaying readings, graphs, and historical usage. Such systems are useful for tracking consumption, but they often provide limited intelligence about the meaning behind the data. A simple increase in electricity usage, for example, may be completely normal during peak operating hours, while the same increase during an otherwise inactive period could indicate unnecessary or inefficient consumption.

This creates a need for a system that can go beyond basic monitoring and help users **understand, interpret, and act on energy consumption data**.

### The Challenge

The proposed problem focuses on addressing the following challenges:

- **Understanding energy usage patterns** across different hours, days, weeks, and operating periods.
- **Predicting future electricity consumption** based on historical consumption behavior and relevant contextual factors.
- **Detecting abnormal or excessive energy usage** by identifying deviations from expected consumption patterns.
- **Identifying potential energy wastage**, such as unusually high consumption during periods of low activity or inactivity.
- **Providing practical insights** that can help users reduce unnecessary energy consumption and electricity costs while maintaining essential operations.

### Why This Problem Matters

Electricity consumption is influenced by many factors, including time of day, occupancy, operating schedules, weather conditions, equipment usage, and user behavior. As a result, the same level of consumption cannot always be considered efficient or inefficient without understanding its context.

For example, high electricity usage during normal working hours may be expected, while similar usage late at night in an otherwise unoccupied building could indicate potential wastage. Without intelligent analysis, such situations can remain unnoticed until they contribute to significantly higher electricity bills or long-term inefficiency.

The challenge, therefore, is not simply to **collect or display electricity consumption data**, but to make that data meaningful. An effective system should be capable of distinguishing normal consumption from unusual behavior, identifying patterns over time, and helping users understand where opportunities for energy efficiency may exist.

### Core Objective

The central objective of this problem is to develop an approach for turning raw electricity consumption data into **meaningful energy intelligence**—enabling users and organizations to better understand their consumption behavior, recognize abnormal or potentially wasteful usage, anticipate future demand, and make more informed energy-related decisions.
